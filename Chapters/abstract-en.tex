This work describes the design and implementation of a simulated smart house controlled from
an Android tablet over Bluetooth Low Energy. The house is driven by a Raspberry Pi 3B+ running
a Python program. It reads real sensors (temperature and pressure, illuminance and motion),
simulates the indoor temperature and a thermostat with heating and cooling, automatically
controls a colour light according to the time of day, illuminance and motion, and shows its
state on a display and indicator LEDs. Externally it acts as a BLE peripheral with the standard
Environmental Sensing service and a custom service that carries the state, the settings and
commands as JSON. A mobile application written in Kotlin displays the state and controls the
house. The work covers the wiring, the communication interface, both programs and their
verification by automated tests and on real hardware.
